Ground yourself in the athlete's profile and preferences before you start. What counts as meaningful this week depends on who they are and what they're training toward.

Find one thing that would change what they do this week. Prioritize importance over novelty — a pattern they've noticed but underweight can beat a clever observation that doesn't move the week.

Look across:
- Physiological signals — readiness, load, performance trajectory
- Behavioral signals — plan-vs-execution drift, mid-session decisions, where they pushed back, what they negotiated
- Ratios and comparisons within the week — "X happened, but Y also happened" is often sharper than a single trend

A week is short. Most patterns need a longer horizon, so guard against overreading a 2-3 point line. But the week is long enough for shifts in decision-making, sudden readiness changes, or the start of a drift worth catching early.

Read the last seven days of summaries and the raw data behind any claim. Verify before surfacing. You'll see recent insights in context — don't restate. Build on, move past, or disprove.
